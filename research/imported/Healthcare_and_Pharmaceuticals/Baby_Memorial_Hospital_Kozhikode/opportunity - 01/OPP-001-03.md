# 2. Direct Quantitative Evidence

The BMH study included:

```text
76 patients
```

treated with the mobile-app strategy.

Historical comparison:

```text
100 patients
```

The results were:

| Metric | Historical group | App group |
|---|---:|---:|
| Patients | 100 | 76 |
| Mean door-to-needle time | 57 min | 41 min |
| Treated within 60 min | 67% | 89% |
| Treated within 45 min | 47% | 57% |
| Mean difference | — | 16 min reduction |

The adjusted difference was statistically significant:

```text
p = 0.005
```

Source:

https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32992177/

The paper concludes that the mobile app improved door-to-needle time as a tool for improving team performance.

**Status: VERIFIED.**

---

# 3. The 16-Minute Reduction

The direct measured change is:

```text
57 min
   ↓
41 min
   =
16 min reduction
```

Relative reduction:

```text
16 / 57 ≈ 28.1%
```

Therefore the app-era mean DNT was approximately 28% lower than the historical comparison mean.

This is a mathematical transformation of the published values.

**Status: VERIFIED / DERIVED.**

But:

> **16 minutes of DNT reduction is not equivalent to 16 minutes of human labour saved.**

The study does not measure the latter.

# 36. Integrity Statement

> **OPP-001-03 establishes that the Baby Memorial Hospital stroke intervention produced a verified historical operational improvement: mean door-to-needle time decreased from 57 to 41 minutes, a 16-minute reduction, while treatment within 60 minutes increased from 67% to 89%. The study does not provide staff minutes, follow-up counts, handoffs, escalation frequency or labour cost. Current residual manual effort and current automation gaps are also unknown. Therefore no labour-savings or ROI claim is justified. ART Fit = MAYBE and Decision = HOLD. The next checkpoint is OPP-001-04 — Direct Operational Evidence / Pain Validation.**
