# 1. Why This Is a New Opportunity

OPP-005 was killed after testing Major Incident Management.

OPP-006 therefore starts from a different business workflow:

> **Field Service Management — exception-driven work allocation and service recovery.**

This is deliberately not an extension of major-incident management.

The initial domain is:

```text
CUSTOMER / SERVICE REQUEST
        ↓
FIELD WORK ORDER
        ↓
TECHNICIAN / CREW / PARTS / LOCATION
        ↓
SCHEDULE
        ↓
FIELD EXECUTION
        ↓
EXCEPTION
        ↓
RE-ALLOCATION / RESCHEDULING / RECOVERY
```

The objective is **not** to claim that ServiceNow has a Field Service gap.

The objective is to determine whether there is a specific consequential decision inside field-service exceptions that survives ServiceNow's current native capabilities.

---

Field Service Management involves physical resources, geographic constraints, skills, customer commitments, inventory, schedules, and changing conditions.

ServiceNow itself identifies field-service challenges including:

# 13. Candidate Decision Boundary

Because broad scheduling is already well supported, the investigation should move one layer deeper.

A candidate residual decision is:

> **When a field-service task becomes infeasible or materially changes after assignment, can the organization determine the best recovery action when several commitments and constraints conflict?**

Possible state:

```text
TASK ASSIGNED
      ↓
TECHNICIAN BECOMES UNAVAILABLE
OR
PART IS MISSING
OR
CUSTOMER WINDOW CHANGES
OR
TRAVEL TIME CHANGES
OR
NEW HIGH-PRIORITY TASK ARRIVES
      ↓
EXISTING PLAN BECOMES INFEASIBLE
      ↓
RECOVERY OPTIONS
```

Possible options:

```text
REASSIGN
RESCHEDULE
SWAP TECHNICIAN
USE CREW
SOURCE PART
CHANGE CUSTOMER APPOINTMENT
ESCALATE
DEFER LOWER-PRIORITY WORK
```

### Classification

**HYPOTHESIS**

No gap established.

# 14. Why This Is Different From Generic Scheduling

The proposed research boundary is not:

> "Can ServiceNow schedule technicians?"

It clearly can.

It is not:

> "Can ServiceNow reassign technicians?"

It clearly can.

It is not:

> "Can ServiceNow respond to a technician calling in sick?"

Current Schedule Optimization explicitly addresses this type of disruption.

Source:

https://www.servicenow.com/docs/r/field-service-management/field-service-scheduling/schedule-optimization-engine.html

The research question is narrower:

> **When multiple business commitments become mutually incompatible, does the recovery decision require reasoning that exceeds the configured scheduling objectives and constraints?**

That is currently only a hypothesis.

# 28. Falsification Rules

OPP-006 should be killed if:

1. the real problem is simply technician scheduling;
2. dynamic scheduling already resolves it;
3. the trade-off can be fully expressed through existing objectives and constraints;
4. the issue is only poor configuration;
5. no real multi-objective conflict can be reconstructed;
6. no material business consequence exists;
7. the decision is actually handled by another control system rather than ServiceNow;
8. no evidence shows recurring decision difficulty.
