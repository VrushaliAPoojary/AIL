# 1. Research Question

The previous checkpoint narrowed OPP-006 to:

> **When multiple customer commitments become incompatible under constrained field capacity, does the recovery decision require reasoning that exceeds configured scheduling objectives and constraints?**

Required evidence chain:

```text
REAL FIELD-SERVICE EVENT
        ↓
INITIAL ASSIGNMENT / PLAN
        ↓
MATERIAL CHANGE
        ↓
CONFLICTING COMMITMENTS
        ↓
MULTIPLE FEASIBLE OPTIONS
        ↓
TRADE-OFF
        ↓
DECISION
        ↓
OUTCOME
```

This checkpoint searches for real-world evidence of that decision problem.

---

# 2. Evidence Strategy

A public ServiceNow customer story rarely exposes the complete internal dispatch decision trail.

Therefore this checkpoint uses two evidence layers:

### Layer A — ServiceNow customer evidence

Used to establish that the target workflow exists in real ServiceNow environments.

### Layer B — Closest credible external operational evidence

Used to establish that the specific field-service conflict occurs in real operations.

The second layer **does not prove a ServiceNow gap**.

It only proves that the operational decision itself is real.

# 3. Strong Real-World Evidence — Ausgrid

Microsoft's official customer story for Ausgrid provides unusually direct evidence of a field-service scheduling conflict.

Ausgrid describes a situation where schedulers needed to dispatch an urgent unplanned job but could not see the planned work already assigned to field technicians.

Source:

https://www.microsoft.com/en/customers/story/25279-ausgrid-dynamics-365-field-service

```text

        ↓

        ↓
CANCELLED WORK WAITED WEEKS FOR RESCHEDULING
        ↓
PRODUCTIVITY / EFFICIENCY IMPACT
        ↓
CUSTOMER COMMITMENT IMPACT
```

Source:

https://www.microsoft.com/en/customers/story/25279-ausgrid-dynamics-365-field-service

**VERIFIED EXTERNAL EVIDENCE**

# 4. Why the Ausgrid Event Matters

This is materially stronger than generic claims such as:

> "Field-service schedules change frequently."

The source describes an actual operational conflict:

```text
EXISTING PLANNED WORK
        VS
URGENT NEW WORK
```

The problem was not simply lack of scheduling.

It was inability to see the competing commitments together before making the dispatch decision.

The documented consequence was that high-priority jobs could be cancelled unintentionally, with cancelled work taking weeks to reschedule.

Source:

https://www.microsoft.com/en/customers/story/25279-ausgrid-dynamics-365-field-service

### Classification

**VERIFIED EXTERNAL EVIDENCE**

# 5. Initial Decision Reconstruction

```text
        ↓

```

Then:


# 7. The Important Counter-Evidence

Ausgrid's solution was not a new reasoning engine.

The company implemented Dynamics 365 Field Service so schedulers could see planned work when dispatching urgent jobs.

The resulting workflow allowed field workers who finished early or experienced a cancellation to see nearby jobs and assign themselves available work.

Source:

https://www.microsoft.com/en/customers/story/25279-ausgrid-dynamics-365-field-service

### Result

The real operational problem was significantly reduced through:

```text
UNIFIED SCHEDULE VISIBILITY
+
REAL-TIME FIELD STATUS
+
SELF-SERVICE JOB SELECTION
```

This is important counter-evidence.

It suggests that at least some apparent "decision intelligence" problems are actually **context-integration and visibility problems**.

# 24. Hypothesis Revision

The original hypothesis was:

> **ServiceNow may not adequately resolve conflicting customer commitments under constrained field capacity.**

After this checkpoint, that is too broad.

A more defensible hypothesis is:

> **Some field-service environments may contain high-consequence conflicts where configured scheduling objectives and constraints do not fully capture business consequences, requiring human judgment beyond optimization.**

### Classification

**HYPOTHESIS**

This remains unvalidated.

---

# 26. What Would Kill the Hypothesis?

OPP-006 should be killed if:

1. all observed conflicts are resolved through objective/constraint configuration;
2. optimization engines already solve the trade-offs;
3. the remaining human work is simply approval;
4. the real issue is missing data;
5. the issue is poor process configuration;
6. another control system already owns the decision;
7. no material customer/business consequence exists;
8. no ServiceNow customer evidence demonstrates the residual burden.

# 30. Decision

# CONTINUE INVESTIGATION — NARROW FURTHER

OPP-006 survives because we now have genuine evidence of the underlying operational conflict.

But the evidence also gives us strong counter-evidence:

> **Better visibility and optimization can eliminate much of the conflict without a specialized reasoning engine.**

Therefore the next checkpoint should not ask whether field-service scheduling is difficult.

That is already proven.

It should ask:

> **When the optimization engine produces multiple operationally feasible schedules, does a human dispatcher still have to make a business judgment that cannot be expressed through the configured objectives and constraints?**

That is the remaining decision boundary.

# 35. Research Integrity Note

This checkpoint deliberately distinguishes:

### REAL OPERATIONAL PROBLEM

Field-service organizations can face real conflicts between urgent work, planned work, customer commitments, resource constraints, and changing field conditions.

**VERIFIED.**

### IMPLEMENTATION SOLUTION

Integrated visibility, optimization, real-time field data, and customer communication can materially reduce these conflicts.

**VERIFIED.**

### SPECIALIZED DECISION GAP

A human dispatcher may still need to choose between multiple feasible schedules based on business consequences that are not fully encoded in the optimization model.

**HYPOTHESIS ONLY.**

Therefore OPP-006 remains open, but the next checkpoint must prove the **residual judgment problem**, not simply repeat evidence that field-service scheduling is complex.
