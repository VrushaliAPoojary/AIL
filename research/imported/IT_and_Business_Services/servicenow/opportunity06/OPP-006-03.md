---

# 1. Research Question

OPP-006-02 established that field-service conflicts are real.

The remaining question is narrower:

> **When multiple schedules are feasible, but each produces a different business outcome, does a dispatcher still need to make a judgment that cannot be adequately expressed through ServiceNow's configured objectives and constraints?**

Required chain:

```text
MULTIPLE FEASIBLE OPTIONS
        ↓
DIFFERENT BUSINESS CONSEQUENCES
        ↓
OBJECTIVES CONFLICT
        ↓
DISPATCHER JUDGMENT
        ↓
DECISION
        ↓
OUTCOME
```

---

# 2. Evidence Standard

This checkpoint distinguishes:

- **DIRECT EVIDENCE** — ServiceNow documentation.
- **VERIFIED EXTERNAL EVIDENCE** — credible field-service research or operational sources.
- **INFERENCE** — interpretation of the evidence.
- **HYPOTHESIS** — not established.
- **UNKNOWN** — evidence unavailable.

No external field-service case study is treated as proof of a ServiceNow customer problem.

---

# 3. Strongest External Evidence — Customer Time vs Provider Cost

A peer-reviewed research article, *Focusing on Customer Time in Field Service: A Normative Approach*, explicitly studies the trade-off between customer convenience and field-service provider cost.

Source:

https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1937-5956.2007.tb00175.x

The paper states that customer convenience should be central to field-service operations and investigates the explicit inclusion of customer time in nonemergency field-service decisions together with the trade-off between customer convenience and provider cost.

### Classification

**VERIFIED EXTERNAL EVIDENCE**

---

# 4. What This Proves

This is stronger than simply saying field-service scheduling has multiple objectives.

It establishes a genuine business trade-off:

```text
CUSTOMER CONVENIENCE
        VS
PROVIDER COST
```

A schedule can therefore be operationally feasible while still producing different business outcomes.

### Classification

**VERIFIED EXTERNAL EVIDENCE**

---

# 5. Stronger Multi-Objective Evidence

A peer-reviewed open-access study in *Computers & Operations Research* describes field-service scheduling as involving multiple objectives including:

- cost;
- task priorities;
- consumer satisfaction.

Source:

https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cor.2021.105472

The study describes a two-level optimization approach in which tasks are assigned to time intervals and then precisely scheduled and routed.

### Classification

**VERIFIED EXTERNAL EVIDENCE**

---

# 6. Critical Finding

The research therefore confirms:

```text
FIELD-SERVICE SCHEDULING
        ↓
MULTIPLE OBJECTIVES
        ↓
OBJECTIVES CAN CONFLICT
        ↓
OPTIMIZATION IS REQUIRED
```

This is not merely an anecdotal operational problem.

It is a recognized field-service optimization problem.

### But this is not yet a ServiceNow gap.

---

# 7. Industry Evidence — Dispatchers Face Competing Objectives

A published study on data-driven field-service planning reports that dispatchers struggle with multiple competing objectives including:

- technician utilization;
- response time;
- customer satisfaction.

Source:

https://www.researchgate.net/publication/333718957_Redesigning_Service_Operations_for_the_Digital_World_Towards_Automated_and_Data-driven_Field_Service_Planning

The study describes approaches that consolidate competing objectives into an optimization problem and support dynamic rescheduling when conditions change.

### Classification

**VERIFIED EXTERNAL EVIDENCE**

---

# 8. Important Counter-Evidence

The same research points toward optimization rather than an unavoidable human-reasoning bottleneck.

The documented approach is:

```text
COMPETING OBJECTIVES
        ↓
FORMALIZE OBJECTIVES
        ↓
OPTIMIZATION
        ↓
DYNAMIC RESCHEDULING
```

Therefore:

> **The existence of competing objectives does not itself establish the need for a separate reasoning layer.**

### Classification

**VERIFIED EXTERNAL EVIDENCE**

---

# 9. ServiceNow's Native Objective Model

ServiceNow's current Schedule Optimization explicitly separates:

### Objectives

Soft goals that influence which schedule is preferred.

### Constraints

Hard rules that an assignment must satisfy.

Source:

https://www.servicenow.com/docs/r/field-service-management/hard-soft-constraints.html

ServiceNow states that objectives are weighted, with higher weights receiving greater priority.

Source:

https://www.servicenow.com/docs/r/field-service-management/hard-soft-constraints.html

### Classification

**DIRECT EVIDENCE**

- minimize travel time;
- maximize high-priority task assignments;
- minimize SLA violations;

- minimize delayed tasks;

Source:

https://www.servicenow.com/docs/r/field-service-management/hard-soft-constraints.html

# 15. The Actual Residual Question

At this point, the hypothesis cannot be:

> "ServiceNow cannot handle competing objectives."

That is disproven.

The remaining question is:

> **What happens when the correct objective weights themselves depend on contextual business judgment that is not known when the policy is configured?**

Example:

```text
NORMAL DAY
MINIMIZE TRAVEL
        ↓
EMERGENCY DAY
MAXIMIZE RESPONSE
        ↓
VIP CUSTOMER ESCALATION
CUSTOMER RETENTION BECOMES MORE IMPORTANT
        ↓
MAJOR PART SHORTAGE
FIRST-TIME-FIX MAY BECOME MORE IMPORTANT
```

### Classification

**HYPOTHESIS**

This is the first genuinely interesting residual boundary.

---

# 16. Why This Is Different

The issue is no longer optimization.

It is:

```text
WHAT SHOULD THE OBJECTIVE WEIGHTS BE
RIGHT NOW?
```

ServiceNow can optimize according to configured objectives.

The unresolved question is whether an external reasoning layer is needed to determine when the business policy itself should change.

### Classification

**INFERENCE**

---

# 17. Strong Counter-Evidence

ServiceNow's documentation explicitly says policies define the rules and priorities guiding Schedule Optimization.

Source:

https://www.servicenow.com/docs/r/field-service-management/field-service-scheduling/create-policies-schedule-optimization.html

Therefore an organization can create different policies for different operating conditions.

This weakens the hypothesis that ServiceNow has no way to adapt business priorities.

### Important implication

A sophisticated organization could potentially implement:

```text
NORMAL POLICY
        ↓
EMERGENCY POLICY
        ↓
HIGH-VALUE CUSTOMER POLICY
        ↓
CAPACITY-CONSTRAINED POLICY
```

without introducing a new intelligence engine.

### Classification

**INFERENCE**


```text
SUPPLY SHORTAGE
        ↓

        ↓

```

Or:

```text
VIP CUSTOMER ESCALATION
        ↓

        ↓

```

Or:

```text

        ↓
RESPONSE TIME BECOMES DOMINANT
        ↓
NORMAL UTILIZATION POLICY MAY BE INAPPROPRIATE
```

The question is:

> **Who or what determines that policy shift?**

This remains unresolved.

---

# 29. Why We Must Not Jump to a Solution

It would be premature to say:

> "This is where a specialized reasoning engine belongs."

Because alternative explanations exist:

1. policy administrators may already maintain multiple policies;
2. dispatch managers may manually select the appropriate policy;
3. business rules may trigger policy changes;
4. the actual control may live outside ServiceNow;
5. the business may not consider the policy-selection problem material.

All remain possible.
