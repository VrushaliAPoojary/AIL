# 1. Research Question

OPP-006-03 narrowed the surviving hypothesis to:

> **When operating context changes materially, the scheduling policy itself may need to change. The unresolved question is who or what determines that policy shift.**

Required chain:

```text
NORMAL OPERATING CONTEXT
        ↓
MATERIAL CONTEXT CHANGE
        ↓
CURRENT POLICY BECOMES LESS SUITABLE
        ↓
ALTERNATIVE POLICY EXISTS
        ↓
POLICY SELECTION
        ↓
REOPTIMIZATION
        ↓
OUTCOME
```

This checkpoint tests whether that pattern is real and whether ServiceNow has a meaningful residual gap at the policy-selection boundary.

---

# 2. Evidence Standard

This checkpoint separates:

- **DIRECT EVIDENCE** — ServiceNow documentation.
- **VERIFIED EXTERNAL EVIDENCE** — credible external operational or product evidence.
- **CLAIM** — assertion made by a source but not independently established.
- **INFERENCE** — interpretation derived from evidence.
- **HYPOTHESIS** — unresolved proposition.
- **UNKNOWN** — evidence unavailable.
- **UNVALIDATED** — commercially or operationally unproven.

# 6. Native Evidence — Policy as Business Rules

ServiceNow defines a policy as a grouping of objectives and constraints that determines how tasks are assigned.

Source:

https://www.servicenow.com/docs/r/field-service-management/field-service-scheduling/create-policies-schedule-optimization.html

The objectives have weights, and higher-weight objectives receive greater priority.

Source:

https://www.servicenow.com/docs/r/field-service-management/hard-soft-constraints.html

Therefore:

```text
BUSINESS PRIORITY
        ↓
POLICY
        ↓
OBJECTIVES + WEIGHTS
+
CONSTRAINTS
        ↓
OPTIMIZATION
```

### Classification

**DIRECT EVIDENCE**

# 17. But What Does ServiceNow Actually Automate?

This is where the evidence becomes decisive.

ServiceNow automatically responds to many scheduling disruptions.

Source:

https://www.servicenow.com/docs/r/field-service-management/field-service-scheduling/configure-intraday-optimization.html

But its current on-demand policy-selection workflow explicitly puts policy choice with the dispatcher.

Source:

https://www.servicenow.com/docs/r/field-service-management/field-service-scheduling/configure-on-demand-optimization.html

Therefore:

```text
EVENT DETECTION
        ↓
AUTOMATED
```

while:

```text
WHICH POLICY SHOULD APPLY?
        ↓
DISPATCHER
```

at least in the documented on-demand workflow.

### Classification

**DIRECT EVIDENCE**

---

# 18. Is This a Capability Gap?

Not yet.

The missing capability might be:

```text
AUTOMATIC POLICY SELECTION
```

But we cannot call that a gap merely because a human performs it.

A legitimate gap requires evidence that:

1. policy selection is difficult;
2. policy selection is recurring;
3. policy selection has material consequences;
4. existing policy rules are insufficient;
5. customers want this decision automated;
6. human selection creates measurable cost, delay, error, or risk.

We currently have evidence for **#1 only at a general industry level**, and even that is not specific to ServiceNow customers.

---

# 19. Strong Counter-Evidence

The strongest counter-evidence is that organizations can simply predefine policies:

```text
NORMAL
HIGH VOLUME
EMERGENCY
OVERTIME
SPECIAL TERRITORY
```

and let dispatchers select the appropriate policy.

Salesforce explicitly documents this model.

Source:

https://trailhead.salesforce.com/content/learn/modules/field-service-lightning-scheduling-basics/customize-a-scheduling-policy

ServiceNow also supports multiple policies and dispatcher selection.

Source:

https://www.servicenow.com/docs/r/field-service-management/field-service-scheduling/configure-on-demand-optimization.html

### Implication

The problem may be operationally solved through:

> **policy catalog + dispatcher judgment**

rather than:

> **autonomous policy arbitration engine**

---

# 20. What We Still Do Not Know

We do not have evidence showing:

- how often ServiceNow customers change policies;
- how long policy-selection decisions take;
- how often the wrong policy is selected;
- whether policy selection causes SLA failures;
- whether policy selection causes overtime or customer churn;
- whether dispatchers lack sufficient context;
- whether multiple policies become difficult to distinguish at scale;
- whether policy selection could be safely automated;
- whether customers would pay for such automation.

### Classification

**UNKNOWN**

# 21. Critical Opportunity Test

The surviving hypothesis is now:

> **In complex field-service operations, dispatchers may struggle to determine which scheduling policy should apply when the operating context changes, creating material decision burden that current ServiceNow workflows do not adequately automate.**

### Status

# HYPOTHESIS — NOT VALIDATED

# 22. What Would Prove It?

We need a real case showing:

```text
CONTEXT CHANGE
        ↓
MULTIPLE POLICIES AVAILABLE
        ↓
POLICY CHOICE IS NON-OBVIOUS
        ↓
DISPATCHER MUST INTERPRET
MULTIPLE BUSINESS SIGNALS
        ↓
DECISION TAKES TIME / CREATES RISK
        ↓
OUTCOME
```

Strong evidence would include:

- emergency classification;
- customer tier;
- SLA exposure;
- workforce shortage;
- weather;
- inventory/parts;
- geographic concentration;
- overtime cost;
- revenue/customer value;
- first-time-fix probability.

The key is not the number of signals.

The key is:

> **Did the dispatcher need to reason across them to choose the policy?**

---

# 23. What Would Kill It?

OPP-006 should be killed if evidence shows:

### Kill condition A

Policies are selected by simple deterministic triggers.

Example:

```text
EMERGENCY = TRUE
        ↓
EMERGENCY POLICY
```

### Kill condition B

Dispatchers simply follow a documented policy matrix.

### Kill condition C

The number of policies is small and selection is trivial.

### Kill condition D

Wrong policy selection has no material consequence.

### Kill condition E

The policy-selection problem is already automated through existing ServiceNow configuration or customer-specific workflows.

### Kill condition F

No ServiceNow customer evidence demonstrates the problem.

# 31. Research Integrity Note

This checkpoint produced an important distinction.

The research **does prove**:

> Context-dependent policy selection is a real field-service operating pattern.

It also **directly proves**:

> ServiceNow already allows multiple scheduling policies and allows dispatchers to change policy before on-demand optimization to adapt to current conditions.

Therefore the opportunity cannot be:

> "ServiceNow lacks policy switching."

That hypothesis is killed.

The only remaining question is much narrower:

> **Is selecting the right policy from complex, changing business context itself a sufficiently difficult, frequent, consequential decision that existing ServiceNow mechanisms do not adequately support?**

That remains **unknown**.

We should not introduce a specialized intelligence layer until that question survives a serious complexity test.
