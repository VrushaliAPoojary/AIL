# 1. Research Question

OPP-006-04 established that context-dependent policy selection is a real field-service pattern and that ServiceNow already supports multiple policies and dispatcher policy selection.

The remaining question is:

> **Is selecting the correct policy sufficiently complex, contextual, recurring, and consequential that a separate intelligence layer is required beyond existing ServiceNow configuration and dispatcher workflows?**

The test is:

```text
CONTEXT CHANGE
        ↓
POLICY A / B / C
        ↓
CAN A SIMPLE RULE CHOOSE?
        ↓
CAN A DECISION TABLE CHOOSE?
        ↓
CAN EXISTING SERVICENOW CONFIGURATION CHOOSE?
        ↓
OR DOES IT REQUIRE CONTEXTUAL BUSINESS JUDGMENT?
```

---

# 2. Evidence Standard

This checkpoint distinguishes:

- **DIRECT EVIDENCE** — current ServiceNow documentation.
- **VERIFIED EXTERNAL EVIDENCE** — credible external research or operational documentation.
- **CLAIM** — assertion made by a source.
- **INFERENCE** — interpretation derived from evidence.
- **HYPOTHESIS** — unresolved proposition.
- **UNKNOWN** — not established.
- **UNVALIDATED** — commercially or operationally unproven.

# 3. Test 1 — Can ServiceNow Express Different Business Priorities?

## Result

# YES

ServiceNow Schedule Optimization policies are explicitly built from objectives and constraints.

Objectives have weights, and higher-weight objectives receive greater priority.

Source:

https://www.servicenow.com/docs/r/field-service-management/field-service-scheduling/create-policies-schedule-optimization.html

The current objectives include, among others:

- maximize task assignment;
- minimize travel;
- maximize high-priority assignments;
- minimize SLA violations;
- minimize overtime;
- maximize preferred-agent assignments;
- maximize existing assignments;
- minimize delayed tasks;
- minimize unassigned tasks.

Source:

https://www.servicenow.com/docs/r/field-service-management/hard-soft-constraints.html

### Classification

**DIRECT EVIDENCE**

### Consequence

Many apparent policy-selection decisions can be reduced to changing objective priorities.

# 8. Test 6 — Can Hard Constraints Separate Feasible From Infeasible Options?

## Result

# YES

ServiceNow distinguishes objectives from constraints.

Objectives are soft targets.

Constraints are mandatory assignment conditions.

Source:

https://www.servicenow.com/docs/r/field-service-management/hard-soft-constraints.html

This creates:

```text
HARD CONSTRAINTS
        ↓
REMOVE INFEASIBLE OPTIONS
        ↓
SOFT OBJECTIVES
        ↓
RANK / PREFER FEASIBLE OPTIONS
```

### Classification

**DIRECT EVIDENCE**

### Consequence

A policy-selection problem cannot be justified merely by saying that multiple possible schedules exist.

The platform already performs feasibility filtering and preference optimization.

# 15. The Remaining Hypothesis

The hypothesis has now become:

> **Some complex field-service environments may contain context combinations that cannot be represented economically through a finite set of policies, rules, objectives, weights, constraints, and overrides, creating recurring human policy-selection burden.**

### Classification

**HYPOTHESIS**

This is narrower than the original OPP-006 hypothesis.

---

# 16. What Would Make This Hypothesis Credible?

We would need evidence of:

```text
MANY CONTEXT VARIABLES
        +
MANY POSSIBLE POLICY STATES
        ↓
POLICY MATRIX BECOMES HARD TO MAINTAIN
        ↓
DISPATCHER MUST INTERPRET CONTEXT
        ↓
DECISION IS RECURRING
        ↓
DECISION IS MATERIAL
```

Strong evidence would show:

- policy proliferation;
- frequent exceptions;
- contradictory rules;
- manual escalation;
- repeated dispatcher overrides;
- policy-selection errors;
- measurable SLA or cost consequences;
- inability to encode business context cleanly.

---

# 17. What Would Kill It?

The hypothesis should be killed if:

### Kill condition 1

A finite policy matrix covers the meaningful operating states.

### Kill condition 2

Simple deterministic triggers select the correct policy.

### Kill condition 3

Existing ServiceNow configuration handles the required context.

### Kill condition 4

Dispatchers rarely need to choose among policies.

### Kill condition 5

Policy selection has negligible business consequences.

### Kill condition 6

The remaining complexity is merely poor implementation/configuration.

### Kill condition 7

No ServiceNow customer evidence demonstrates recurring policy-selection difficulty.

# 19. Important Architectural Finding

This checkpoint produces a useful architectural distinction:

```text
LEVEL 1 — EVENT DETECTION
        ↓
WHAT CHANGED?

LEVEL 2 — FEASIBILITY
        ↓
WHAT CAN STILL BE DONE?

LEVEL 3 — OPTIMIZATION
        ↓
WHAT IS THE BEST SCHEDULE UNDER CURRENT POLICY?

LEVEL 4 — POLICY SELECTION
        ↓
WHICH POLICY SHOULD APPLY?

LEVEL 5 — BUSINESS GOVERNANCE
        ↓
SHOULD THE POLICY ITSELF CHANGE?
```

ServiceNow has strong documented capability across Levels 1–3 and explicit human mechanisms at Level 4.

The potential residual research area is Level 5.

### Classification

**INFERENCE**

This is not evidence of a ServiceNow gap.

# 28. Research Integrity Note

This checkpoint deliberately refuses to turn "AI could choose the policy" into an opportunity.

The evidence establishes:

- field-service environments have changing operating contexts;
- multiple scheduling policies are legitimate;
- competing objectives are real;
- ServiceNow provides objectives, weights, constraints, policies, overrides, automatic optimization, and dispatcher control.

The evidence does **not** establish:

- that ServiceNow customers cannot configure their required policies;
- that policy selection is a recurring major burden;
- that dispatchers make frequent costly policy-selection mistakes;
- that an autonomous policy-arbitration layer is demanded;
- that customers would pay for one.

Therefore OPP-006 remains only a **weak research hypothesis**.

The next checkpoint must seek direct or exceptionally strong external evidence of the residual burden.

If that evidence cannot be found, the correct outcome is **KILL**, not another technical hypothesis.
