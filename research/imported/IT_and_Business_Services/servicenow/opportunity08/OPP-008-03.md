# 1. Research Question

OPP-008-02 reconstructed a documented hospital patient-flow decision:

```text
PATIENT
↓
EXPECTED DISCHARGE TODAY
↓
LABORATORY TASK OUTSTANDING
↓
DISCHARGE AT RISK
↓
COMMAND CENTRE IDENTIFIES BARRIER
↓
LABORATORY PRIORITIZES TASK
↓
PATIENT PROGRESSES
```

The question for this checkpoint is:

> **Can the strongest reasonable ServiceNow-native architecture represent and execute this operational decision using existing healthcare workflows, cases, task routing, AI agents, resource/capacity capabilities, and integrations?**

The objective is to try to **kill** the opportunity, not to prove it.

# 15. The Critical Distinction

The reconstructed workflow contains two different problems.

## Problem A — Coordination

```text
KNOWN BLOCKER
        ↓
FIND OWNER
        ↓
ROUTE
        ↓
ESCALATE
        ↓
COMPLETE
```

ServiceNow appears well positioned here.

## Problem B — Decision synthesis

```text
MULTIPLE PATIENTS
+
MULTIPLE BLOCKERS
+
MULTIPLE RESOURCES
+
MULTIPLE CAPACITY CONSTRAINTS
+
TIME PRESSURE
        ↓
WHICH ACTION SHOULD HAPPEN FIRST?
```

This is less clearly covered.

### Classification

**A = STRONG NATIVE CAPABILITY**

**B = UNKNOWN**

---

# 16. Why This Matters

If the customer only needs:

> "Make sure the lab completes the discharge-blocking task."

then ServiceNow workflow is likely sufficient.

If the customer needs:

> "Continuously evaluate hundreds of interacting patient-flow constraints and determine which operational intervention produces the best system-level result."

then the problem is materially different.

### Classification

**INFERENCE**

The evidence does not yet establish that ServiceNow customers actually require the second capability.

# 19. Surviving Hypothesis

A narrower hypothesis survives:

> **Hospital-wide patient-flow decision synthesis may remain a residual problem where existing healthcare workflow systems provide data and coordination but do not automatically reconcile multiple interacting operational constraints into a system-level recommended intervention.**

### Classification

# HYPOTHESIS

This is not a finding.

---

# 20. What Evidence Would Kill This Hypothesis?

The hypothesis should be killed if we find evidence that:

1. ServiceNow Healthcare Operations already has native patient-flow optimization.
2. ServiceNow customers can configure existing AI agents to perform this decision synthesis.
3. ServiceNow's platform can already integrate the required data and run adequate optimization through existing modules.
4. Hospitals commonly use external patient-flow optimizers and ServiceNow simply orchestrates their outputs.
5. Human command-centre staff do not experience material decision burden.
6. No measurable operational benefit exists from better prioritization.

---

# 21. What Evidence Would Strengthen It?

Evidence would strengthen the hypothesis if:

```text
REAL HOSPITAL
        ↓
REAL COMMAND CENTRE
        ↓
MULTIPLE SIMULTANEOUS BOTTLENECKS
        ↓
MULTIPLE POSSIBLE INTERVENTIONS
        ↓
HUMAN TEAM MUST SYNTHESIZE
DATA FROM MULTIPLE SYSTEMS
        ↓
REPEATED MANUAL REASONING
        ↓
MATERIAL DELAY / CAPACITY LOSS
        ↓
NO EXISTING SYSTEM
ADEQUATELY RECOMMENDS
THE BEST OPERATIONAL ACTION
```

This is the evidence we still need.
