# 1. Research Question

OPP-008-04 established a narrow hypothesis:

> Hospital command centres may face a recurring decision-synthesis burden when multiple patient-flow constraints interact.

This checkpoint asks:

> **When hospital teams choose between competing patient-flow interventions, what criteria actually determine the decision, where is authority located, and when does the decision move from local judgment to central reprioritization?**

The objective is to reconstruct the decision logic before making any claim about a technology gap.

---

# 2. Primary Evidence

The principal source is a 2026 multiple-case study of five academic hospitals based on site visits and 157 interviews with 112 informants.

Source:

https://doi.org/10.1080/09537287.2026.2655748

The study identified approximately 70–90 relevant patient-flow decisions per hospital and categorized them according to where and how decisions were made.

### Classification

**VERIFIED EXTERNAL EVIDENCE**

---

# 3. The Core Finding

The study does **not** show one universal hospital decision rule.

Instead, it identifies four empirically associated decision modes:

```text
LOCAL + DETAILED
        ↓
LOCAL DETAILED CONTROL

LOCAL + AGGREGATED
        ↓
LOCAL AGGREGATED REBALANCING

CENTRAL + AGGREGATED
        ↓
CENTRALLY AGGREGATED PLANNING

CENTRAL + DETAILED
        ↓
CENTRALLY DETAILED REPRIORITIZATION
```

Source:

https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/09537287.2026.2655748

### Classification

**VERIFIED EXTERNAL EVIDENCE**

This is more important than a generic "AI could optimize hospital flow" hypothesis because it identifies the actual organizational structure of the decisions.

# 34. Research Integrity Note

This checkpoint materially changes the hypothesis.

We now know that the hospital decision is **not** a simple optimization problem with a universal score.

The evidence shows a layered decision architecture:

```text
LOCAL CONTEXT
        +
SYSTEM STATE
        +
DEMAND / CAPACITY
        +
INTERDEPENDENCE
        +
VOLATILITY
        ↓
DECIDE WHERE AUTHORITY SHOULD SIT
        ↓
LOCAL CONTROL
OR
LOCAL REBALANCING
OR
CENTRAL PLANNING
OR
CENTRAL REPRIORITIZATION
```

This is important because it means a future specialized system would have to understand **decision authority and context**, not merely rank tasks.

At the same time, existing hospitals already use predictive tools, command centres and specialized optimization.

Therefore the remaining research question is exceptionally narrow:

> **Does a material, repeatable cross-system synthesis problem remain between existing specialized decision-support tools and the final command-centre decision?**

Until that is demonstrated, there is **no capability gap, no customer opportunity, and no AIL intake**.
