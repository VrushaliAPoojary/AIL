# 1. Research Question

The surviving OPP-008 hypothesis entering this checkpoint was:

> **Some hospital command centres may retain a material cross-system decision-synthesis burden when data from specialized systems are incomplete, conflicting, or context-dependent and the final intervention requires human reconciliation across organizational boundaries.**

This checkpoint required a stronger standard:

```text
REAL OPERATIONAL EVENT
        ↓
MULTIPLE DATA SOURCES
        ↓
CONFLICT / MISSING / STALE INFORMATION
        ↓
HUMAN RECONCILIATION
        ↓
EXPLICIT DECISION CRITERIA
        ↓
TRADE-OFF
        ↓
ACTION
        ↓
OUTCOME
```

The purpose was to determine whether a **real documented decision** could be reconstructed rather than continuing to infer the opportunity from general command-centre descriptions.

---

# 2. Decision Rule

The opportunity would survive only if the public evidence established enough of the chain to demonstrate:

1. a real operational event;
2. multiple information sources;
3. an actual discrepancy, conflict, missing/stale information, or incompatible system state;
4. human reconciliation of that problem;
5. an explicit operational decision;
6. a meaningful trade-off;
7. an observable action or outcome.

If this evidence could not be established, the residual hypothesis would be killed rather than strengthened through inference.

---

# 3. Evidence Finding 1 — Real Seven-System Fragmentation

Johns Hopkins Hospital's Capacity Command Center was initially designed around patient-flow and hospital-capacity information distributed across **seven unique systems**, most of which did not interface with one another.

The authors report that the information could be accessed, but doing so required significant effort and time.

The command centre subsequently integrated feeds from relevant sources into real-time analytical displays.

Source:

https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jcjq.2018.11.006

Supporting source:

https://www.gehccommandcenter.com/hubfs/CC2020/docs/JCJQPS-Johns-Hopkins-Use-of-Systems-Engineering-to-Design-A-Hospital-Command-Center.pdf

### Classification

# DIRECT VERIFIED EXTERNAL EVIDENCE

### What this proves

```text
MULTIPLE SYSTEMS
+
LOW INTEROPERABILITY
+
HIGH ACCESS EFFORT
        ↓
INTEGRATION PROJECT
```

### What it does NOT prove

It does not document a particular patient decision in which conflicting system information caused a human reconciliation problem.

# 4. Evidence Finding 2 — Real Prescriptive Bed Decision Support

The Johns Hopkins Capacity Command Center evolved from descriptive displays toward predictive and prescriptive analytics.

The published design reports that predictive tiles forecast occupancy and wait times and that prescriptive tiles provide decision support, including a **recommended bed placement**.

Source:

https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jcjq.2018.11.006

### Classification

# DIRECT VERIFIED EXTERNAL EVIDENCE

### Critical implication

This is strong counter-evidence against the proposition:

> "Hospital command centres only aggregate information and humans must manually synthesize the decision."

At least one mature command-centre implementation already included prescriptive decision support for bed placement.

# 5. Evidence Finding 3 — Human Adjudication Still Exists

The Johns Hopkins design also reports that the command-centre medical director participates in day-to-day decisions, including:

- adjudicating disputed patient bed assignments;
- participating in discussions about complex transfers;
- bringing frontline perspective to downstream effects on patient care.

Source:

https://www.researchgate.net/publication/330334261_Use_of_Systems_Engineering_to_Design_a_Hospital_Command_Center

The underlying paper is:

https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jcjq.2018.11.006

### Classification

# DIRECT VERIFIED EXTERNAL EVIDENCE

### What this proves

```text
COMPLEX / DISPUTED CASE
        ↓
CENTRAL HUMAN ADJUDICATION
```

### What it does NOT prove

The source does not specify:

- the exact conflicting data;
- the individual case;
- the competing alternatives;
- the decision criteria;
- the time required;
- the measurable consequence.

Therefore this cannot be upgraded into a complete cross-system reconciliation event.

# 6. Evidence Finding 4 — Real Data Discrepancies and Human Verification

The NHS artificial-intelligence command-centre evaluation provides direct evidence that data quality remained a practical operational issue.

The study reports that:

- electronic records were not always sufficiently up to date, accurate, or complete;
- staff often had to triangulate and verify information from multiple sources and systems;
- discrepancies existed between system data and what frontline staff reported;
- compromise, goodwill, and a shared sense of purpose were necessary for the command centre to function effectively.

Source:

https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK608696/

Supporting ethnographic findings:

https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK608708/

### Classification

# DIRECT VERIFIED EXTERNAL EVIDENCE

This is the strongest evidence found for:

```text
SYSTEM DATA
        ↓
DATA QUALITY PROBLEM
        ↓
MULTIPLE SOURCE VERIFICATION
        ↓
HUMAN JUDGMENT
```

# 7. Critical Limitation of Evidence Finding 4

The NHS evaluation does **not** provide a single patient-flow decision in which:

```text
SYSTEM A = X
SYSTEM B = Y
FRONTLINE REPORT = Z
        ↓
COMMAND CENTRE RECONCILES
        ↓
CHOSES OPTION A OVER B
        ↓
MEASURABLE OUTCOME
```

The study reports the existence of triangulation and discrepancy at the workflow level.

It does not provide the complete event-level decision chain required by this checkpoint.

### Classification

# VERIFIED WORKFLOW PATTERN — NOT VERIFIED DECISION EVENT

This distinction is decisive.

# 11. Evidence Finding 7 — Transfer Disputes

The 2026 study reports a meaningful difference between hospitals in how centrally assigned bed placements are contested.

In some hospitals, central bed managers have strong gatekeeping authority and ward objections must meet strict criteria.

In other hospitals, objections are more common and can become protracted, particularly where physicians have longer windows to contest assignments.

Source:

https://doi.org/10.1080/09537287.2026.2655748

### Classification

# DIRECT VERIFIED EXTERNAL EVIDENCE

```text
CENTRAL PLACEMENT
        ↓
LOCAL OBJECTION
        ↓
NEGOTIATION / ADJUDICATION
```

### But again

It does not document a particular case with:

```text
DATA CONFLICT
+
ALTERNATIVES
+
TRADE-OFF
+
FINAL DECISION
+
OUTCOME
```

Therefore it cannot satisfy the checkpoint's full evidence requirement.

# 16. Falsification Result

The specific hypothesis:

> **A material cross-system reconciliation decision burden remains at hospital command centres and represents a distinct opportunity for a specialized decision-synthesis layer.**

# NOT DEMONSTRATED

Because the required event-level evidence is absent.

Under the investigation rule established at the start of this checkpoint, the hypothesis must therefore be:

# KILLED

This does **not** mean:

> "Cross-system reconciliation never happens."

That would be unsupported.

It means:

> **The public evidence assembled here is insufficient to establish it as a distinct, material, repeatable opportunity.**

Johns Hopkins began with patient-flow and capacity information distributed across seven systems that largely did not interface.


- visibility;
- data quality;

https://doi.org/10.1080/09537287.2026.2655748

```text
RECOMMEND
≠
COMMAND
```

```text
WORKFLOW
+
DECISION
+
ACTION
+
MEASURED OUTCOME
```

This investigation is especially useful for ART because it exposes a dangerous research failure mode:

```text

        ↓

        ↓

        ↓
HYPOTHESIZED GAP
        ↓
```

ART must therefore preserve the distinction between:

```text
PROBLEM EXISTS
```

```text

```

OPP-008 did not cross that boundary.

# 20. AOI Decision

# KILL

Kill **OPP-008 as currently formulated**.

The decision is not based on evidence that hospital command centres are problem-free.

It is based on failure to demonstrate the specific opportunity:

> **a material, repeatable cross-system reconciliation decision gap that existing systems do not adequately address and that a specialized ART layer could defensibly solve.**

Continuing to deepen the same hypothesis from public evidence would now create an increasing risk of turning:

```text
REAL WORKFLOW EVIDENCE
```

into:

```text
ASSUMED CAPABILITY GAP
```

That violates the research contract.
